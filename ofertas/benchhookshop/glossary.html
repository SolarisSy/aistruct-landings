Glossary

A lexicon of the wall

Terms used across Benchhook essays, defined the way we use them

These are working definitions, not industry standards — the way this journal uses the words when we write about fixed positions, shadow boards, and the daily count. Some overlap with shop-floor jargon; a few we coined out of necessity.

Fixed Position
The single, unchanging spot assigned to a tool on a wall or in a drawer, regardless of who used it last.
Shadow Board
A mounting surface with each tool's silhouette painted or cut directly onto it, so an empty space reads at a glance.
Painted Silhouette
The outline of a tool traced and filled or edged onto a board, working as both a storage guide and a passive count.
Return Discipline
The habit, individual or shared, of putting a tool back in its fixed position immediately after use rather than at some later tidy-up.
Closing Count
A short tally run at the end of a shift or day to confirm every fixed position is filled before the bench is left.
Open Outline
A silhouette or labeled space currently missing its tool — the visual signal that prompts a search or a note in the log.
Restock Interval
The typical span of time between one restocking of a consumable or tool and the next, whether measured in days or in uses.
Frequency Sort
Arranging tools by how often they are used rather than by category, material, or alphabet.
Tool Crib
A designated storage area, often shared among several people, where tools are checked out and returned rather than owned individually.
Sign-Out Sheet
A paper or digital log recording who took a tool from a crib and when it is expected back.
Shrinkage
The gradual, often unnoticed loss of tools or stock over time, distinct from a single theft or breakage event.
Drawer Blindness
The tendency for a closed drawer to hide a shortage longer than an open wall would, simply because nothing is visible until it's opened.
Wall Map
A diagram, physical or digital, showing every fixed position on a pegboard or shadow board and what belongs in each.
Hook Spacing
The distance between mounting points on a pegboard, which determines what sizes and shapes of tool can be hung where.
Bench Footprint
The total working area of a bench or station, often used as a rough unit for planning storage density around it.
Handover Slip
A short note passed between shifts confirming the tool count at the start and end of a shift on a shared bench.
Peg Density
How many hooks or fixed positions are packed into a given area of pegboard — a rough measure of storage efficiency.
Consumable Buffer
A small reserve quantity of a wear item — blades, bits, fasteners — kept on hand to cover the gap before restocking arrives.
Silhouette Drift
When a painted outline no longer matches the tool it was meant for, usually after a tool is replaced with a different model.
Visual Audit
A quick, informal inventory check done by eye rather than by ledger, relying on shadow boards or outlines to reveal gaps.
