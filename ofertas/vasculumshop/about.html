Why we keep this journal

Vasculum is written by people who press plants themselves, for people who are learning to, or already do.

The mission

Herbarium practice is old, quiet work that most people encounter once, in a school botany unit, and then forget entirely. We think that's a loss. Pressing a plant well and labeling it properly is a skill anyone can pick up with a few tools and a handful of habits, and the record it produces — a specimen with a real locality, date, and name attached — outlasts almost anything else you might make in an afternoon outdoors.

Vasculum exists to write down the specific, testable parts of that skill: how often to change a blotter, what a label actually needs to say, how to handle a stem that won't flatten. We try to avoid vague encouragement in favor of things you can check against your own press.

A small, fictional editorial team

Vasculum is edited by a rotating handful of contributors who collect and press in different climates — coastal, arid, and temperate — which is partly why entries sometimes disagree slightly on timing. A blotter interval that works in a dry inland press will not match one in a humid coastal room, and we would rather show that variation than flatten it into one false rule.

Entries are written, reviewed by at least one other contributor who has pressed the species or technique in question, and published without bylines attached to individual posts, since most of what we publish is a synthesis of more than one person's notes.

Content policy

  • Everything we publish reflects direct pressing and mounting experience, not summarized secondhand advice.
  • We do not accept sponsored posts, paid placements, or product reviews written by the manufacturer.
  • Species names and localities in the journal are illustrative; we do not publish precise coordinates for rare or protected populations.
  • We correct errors when readers point them out, and we note the correction rather than silently editing a published entry.
  • Vasculum does not sell specimens, seeds, or plant material of any kind.
A specimen without a label is a pressed plant. A specimen with a label is a record.

Get in touch

Questions, corrections, or a technique you think we got wrong — write to hello@vasculum.shop. We read everything, even if a reply takes a while during collecting season.
