Glossary

Terms we use often in the journal, defined the way we use them — not always the strictest botanical definition, but the practical one.

  • Vasculum

    A rigid field case, traditionally tin or metal, used to carry freshly collected plants without crushing them before they reach the press. Many collectors now use a plastic bag or a folder instead, but the term persists for the collecting kit itself.

  • Blotter

    An absorbent sheet, usually corrugated cardboard lined with paper, placed against a specimen inside the press to draw out moisture. Blotters are changed on a schedule until the specimen stops releasing water.

  • Ventilator

    A corrugated sheet placed between blotters, oriented so its channels run open to the edge of the press, allowing air to move through and speed drying.

  • Herbarium sheet

    The standard-size archival paper, roughly 11.5 by 16.5 inches in most collections, to which a dried specimen is permanently mounted along with its label.

  • Determination

    The identification assigned to a specimen, along with who made the call and when. A specimen can carry more than one determination over time as taxonomy or expert opinion changes.

  • Determination slip

    A small strip added below the original label when a specimen is re-identified, rather than altering or removing the first label. Keeps the full identification history intact on the sheet.

  • Locality

    The written description of where a specimen was collected — county, nearest landmark, elevation, habitat — specific enough that someone else could find the same site.

  • Collector's number

    A sequential number a collector assigns to their own specimens, unique to that person's collecting history, used to cross-reference field notes with the finished sheet.

  • Voucher specimen

    A pressed specimen kept as physical proof that a particular plant was observed at a particular place and time, often cited in field surveys or research notes.

  • Duplicate

    A second (or third) specimen collected from the same plant at the same time, often distributed to another herbarium or collector so the record isn't held in only one place.

  • Hinge mounting

    Attaching a specimen to its sheet using small strips of gummed linen tape at a few contact points, rather than adhering the whole plant with glue — reversible and gentler on brittle tissue.

  • Strapping

    Securing heavier or bulkier parts of a specimen — a thick stem, a cone — to the sheet with thin cloth or paper straps sewn or glued only at their ends.

  • Fragment packet

    A small folded paper envelope attached to a herbarium sheet, used to hold loose material — seeds, detached leaves, pollen — that isn't mounted directly.

  • Field press

    A lightweight, often strapless version of a plant press carried during collecting to give specimens an initial flattening before they reach the full press at home.

  • Silica gel drying

    An alternative to traditional blotter pressing, using silica desiccant to rapidly dry a specimen, often chosen for material that discolors badly under slow pressing, such as some flowers.

  • Annotation label

    Any label added to a sheet after the original collection label, recording a loan, a citation in published research, or a note from someone who examined the specimen later.

  • Genus cover

    A folded sheet of heavier stock used to group all specimens of one genus together inside a storage box or cabinet, usually labeled on the fold with the genus name.

  • Type specimen

    The particular specimen formally designated as the permanent reference for a species name when it was first described — kept under stricter handling rules than an ordinary sheet.

  • Accession number

    A unique identifier assigned to a specimen when it enters a collection's permanent record, distinct from the collector's own field number.

  • Pressing weight

    The downward force applied to a press, traditionally from straps tightened by hand or added weight, adjusted so specimens flatten evenly without crushing delicate parts.
