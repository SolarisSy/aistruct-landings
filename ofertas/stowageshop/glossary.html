A Glossary of Household Logistics

Terms we use across the log, defined the way we use them — not always the way a dictionary would.

L-01

Load Order

The sequence in which boxes and furniture go into a truck or van, decided by weight and destination rather than by convenience or room.

L-02

Density Per Box

How much weight sits in a given box relative to its size — a box can look full while still being far from its practical weight limit, or the reverse.

L-03

Weight Class

A rough category — light, medium, heavy, or fragile — assigned to a box to decide where it sits in a stack or a load order.

L-04

Manifest

A written record of what's inside each box, its weight class, and its destination — the household version of a shipping manifest.

L-05

Corridor Route

The single path through a home judged shortest and least obstructed from a given room to the exit, used for repeated box trips.

L-06

Dead Weight

Items whose weight is disproportionate to their size — books, tools, and tile or stone samples are common offenders.

L-07

Fragile Stack Rule

The practice of placing fragile items according to their actual density and support needs, rather than assuming "fragile" always means "goes on top."

L-08

Room-First Packing

The common habit of packing box by box within a single room, without regard to the resulting weight — the pattern this site argues against.

L-09

Staging Zone

A temporary holding area near the exit where boxes wait, sorted, before they're carried out and loaded.

L-10

Load-Bearing Box

A heavy, sturdy box placed at the base of a stack, chosen for its ability to support lighter boxes above it without deforming.

L-11

Void Fill

Any material used to fill empty space inside a box, keeping contents from shifting or settling in transit.

L-12

Tare Weight

The weight of an empty box or container — a useful baseline for estimating how heavy it will be once packed.

L-13

Access Path

The route kept clear of furniture and clutter during the loading window, so carriers can move without rerouting mid-trip.

L-14

Reverse Manifest

The plan for the order boxes should be unloaded and opened at the new home, distinct from the order they were loaded in.

L-15

Zone Labeling

Labeling boxes by functional zone and sequence — for example a short code — instead of a vague room name that says nothing about contents or priority.

L-16

Weight Distribution

Spreading heavy items across several boxes or areas of a load rather than concentrating them in one place.

L-17

First-Night Box

The box packed last and opened first, holding whatever is needed before the rest of the manifest gets unpacked.

L-18

Choke Point

A narrow passage — a doorway, stairwell, or elevator — that limits how quickly and how large a load can move through it.

L-19

Load Plan

The overall strategy for sequencing and arranging a load, combining load order, weight distribution, and route into a single approach.

L-20

Nesting

Packing smaller items inside larger ones — pots inside pots, bowls inside bowls — to use a box's volume more efficiently.
