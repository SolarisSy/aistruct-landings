About

A Notebook, Not an Observatory

Nightglass started as a set of notes kept on a phone during a long stretch of clear winter nights and grew into a small journal. It stays deliberately narrow: one household, one ordinary sky, written down honestly.

Why this journal exists

Most astronomy writing online assumes a dark-sky trip, a dedicated observatory, or gear well past a beginner's budget. That's a real and useful world, but it isn't the one most people actually live in. Nightglass exists for the much larger group of people observing from a porch, a driveway, or a small patch of lawn between two neighbors' floodlights.

The angle is consistent across every piece here: describe what a given target, technique, or piece of gear actually looks like under real suburban glow, not under ideal conditions borrowed from a different sky entirely.

Editorial approach

Every post on this site is written from direct observation or hands-on testing, then checked against public reference material for the underlying astronomy. When something is uncertain — a rise time, a magnitude estimate, a piece of gear advice — we say so, rather than presenting it as settled fact. We do not accept payment for coverage, and we do not run sponsored placements inside articles.

Content policy

  • Original writing only — no syndicated or recycled copy from other outlets.
  • Astronomical claims are framed as observation or estimate, not as guaranteed outcomes, since actual visibility depends on local conditions we can't control for a reader.
  • Gear mentions describe general categories (a wide-field reflector, an 8x42 binocular) rather than pushing a specific purchase.
  • Corrections are made openly; if a post turns out to be wrong about a detail, it gets fixed and noted, not quietly edited.
